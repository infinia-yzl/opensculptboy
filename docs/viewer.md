# Web viewer

`viewer/dist/anny_viewer.html` shapes and poses a character in a browser. It is one HTML file that draws with WebGL2, with skin, eye and hair shading. Open it in a browser; it needs no server, apart from the [AI providers](#ai-providers) panel, which needs the page served at `http://localhost:41730` by `serve_viewer.py`.

## Using it

- The top bar holds the six modes, one per task: **Create** (`C`), **Dress** (`D`), **Express** (`X`), **Pose** (`P`), **Sheet** (`S`) and **Export** (`E`). A mode opens its panel on the left; its key or its button closes the panel again. On a phone the modes sit in a bar at the bottom and the panel is a bottom sheet; between 720 and 1100 pixels they stand in a rail at the left.
- A first visit opens on the Doryphoros, a statue fitted to a scan, when its package lies beside a served page, and on Anny's default body until then ([Starters and the New screen](#starters-and-the-new-screen)), with a welcome card at the side that leaves the page in use. `?base` stays on Anny's default body.
- **Create** holds the body sliders, the 103 face shapes with a **Random face** button, the skin and eyes with the stone library, and 25 hairstyles, as four tabs (`B` Body, `F` Face, `K` Skin and eyes, `H` Hair). The `age` parameter appears as **Form**, with the ends *Compact* and *Elongated*. The panel's foot holds the tab's random draw and reset, and **Use own** on the Skin tab, which makes a statue its own skin again.
- **Dress** holds the wearables of a character card ([Wearables](#wearables)), **Express** the expressions ([Faces](#faces)), **Pose** the poses and clips, **Sheet** the [character sheet](#sheet), and **Export** the files that the page writes: the character card (**Save card**, `Ctrl S`), a picture of the view, the sheet, the pose card and the silhouette. GLB and VRM show as *Planned*, with the command that writes them now from the saved card.
- **New** (`N`) opens the New screen; **Open** (`Ctrl O`) opens a character card with its files, its folder or its `.zip`, the card inside an OpenSculptBoy GLB or VRM file, wearable packages, sheet settings, a character of the page or a picture to pose from; a drop anywhere on the page does the same ([Opening files](#opening-files)).
- The character's button in the top bar opens the **Character** panel: what the character holds (the body, the face, the skin, the eyes, the hair, the outfit and the faces), whether each part comes from the card or from the page's library, and a link to the mode that changes it.
- The **View** menu holds the **Rig** (`R`) and **Scene** (`L`) panels, which open on the right, the **Performance** card, full screen, the theme (light marble, dark marble or the system's setting) and **Settings** with the [AI providers](#ai-providers). In the light theme the 3D view stands on a cool grey stone ground, darker at its edges, so that a pale statue or fair skin stands out by its light and its shade; the figure keeps the lighting preset's exposure, which the sheet's meter targets. Every framing fills the height between the top bar and the view bar, so the head clears the mode bar at every view.
- The Pose section offers **From a picture**: it poses the body, the head and the fingers as the figure in a photo or a drawing ([Pose from a picture](pose-from-picture.md) describes the method). The **Head turn**, **Head up** and **Head tilt** sliders correct the head afterwards. The picture stays in the browser. MediaPipe's WebAssembly and its pose and hand models are files beside the page (`viewer/dist/mediapipe/`), pinned by SHA-256, so From a picture works offline; it needs the page served by `serve_viewer.py`, and on a page opened as a file it says so.
- The camera turns with a drag and zooms with the wheel; the view bar under the figure holds the framings, the angles, the turntable, the reset and the refinement ring. The `?` key lists every shortcut, with a search field. The shortcuts work wherever the focus is, except while a text field or a dialog has it (Esc leaves the field), and on every keyboard: a letter key means its letter with Caps Lock on, and a keyboard in another script or an input method that reports no letter falls back to the key's place on a US keyboard (`shortcutKeys` in `viewer/src/ui/shortcuts.ts`). Every key comes from one table in that file (`MODES`, `SECTIONS`, `TOOLS` and `SHORTCUTS`), which the key handler, the tooltips and the help read. `test.test_viewer_shortcuts` checks the keys in node, and in the built page (local only) presses every shortcut of the help after a person's steps (a card opened through **Open folder**, a slider, a switch, a fold, the help closed by its button, a name typed and left with Esc) and checks its effect.
- The **Performance** card (the `` ` `` key or `?stats=1`) shows the frame times, the GPU time of a frame and runs a 10-second benchmark. [Frame budget](#frame-budget) describes how the page keeps a frame within its GPU time.
- `?shot` hides the whole interface, for screenshots.
- A slider edit builds the body in a Web Worker, so the page keeps drawing during a drag. The worker also holds the local changes and the detail offsets of an opened card. `?bodyworker=off` builds every body on the page, for comparison.

The Pose section saves the pose shown as a character card ([Character card](character-card.md)) for `opensculptboy export`, or as a silhouette SVG; Export offers both too.

## Starters and the New screen

The starters are classical statues fitted to public-domain scans (`viewer/src/starters.ts`): the **Doryphoros**, the spear bearer of Polykleitos with its tree stump and its base, and **David with the head of Goliath**, with his sword, his hat and the head at his feet. Each is a character card with its sculpted detail in one package, `<statue>.zip`, written by `python -m opensculptboy.statues starter`, and stands in the card's own pose. Its props (a base, a stump, what the statue holds) are wearables of the slot `prop`, which sit beside each other as accessories do, and Dress shows them first as switches.

The packages are too large for the repository. They are files of a GitHub release (the tag in `viewer/src/starter_packages.json`), and the page reads them from the folder `starters/` beside it when it is served by `serve_viewer.py`:

```
anny_viewer.html
serve_viewer.py
serve.json
mediapipe/
starters/doryphoros.zip
starters/david_goliath.zip
```

The page pins each package by its size and SHA-256 (`viewer/src/starter_packages.json`) and fetches it only when the person picks its statue, with that hash as the request's integrity, so the browser refuses any other bytes. The New screen tags a statue whose package is not beside the page with **Download**, and picking it shows a note with the package's download link: the package is missing, it is another version than the page pins, or the page was opened as a file and reads nothing beside it. A downloaded package dropped on the page opens as any card's `.zip` does. A first visit opens the Doryphoros when its package is there, and otherwise the welcome card carries its download link.

After a package changes, pin it again with `python viewer/starter_packages.py FOLDER` (`--release TAG` for a new release), which writes `starter_packages.json` and the New screen's pictures (`viewer/src/starter_thumbs.ts`, from each package's `thumbnail.png`), build the page again, and upload the packages to the release of that tag.

### The floor under the props

The page stands the figure on the lowest point of what it shows: Anny's body and the shown props, skinned by the pose (`viewer/src/ground.ts`). A statue's base or a head at its feet stands on the floor, and the body stands on them; when a prop is switched on or off in Dress, the figure moves to the floor again, and the Full framing takes the props' lift into account. `window.groundInfo()` gives the floor, the root's lift and the lowest points of the body and the props for the page tests.

The New screen (`N`) holds the statues and a **Blank body** (Anny's default body in the A pose), the libraries (skin, eyes, hair and wear) with **Start from the base**, and **Your 3D model**, which reads an OpenSculptBoy GLB or VRM file and explains the fit for other models. Its foot offers **Open a card**, **Continue** with the character shown and **Random**.

## The stone library

The Skin and eyes tab's first row of swatches is the stone library (`viewer/src/stones.ts`): Carrara, Travertine, Bardiglio, Basalt, Bronze and Verdigris, and a stone of a colour of its own. A stone makes the character a statue: the skin and the eyes draw as the stone, with its clouds, its veins and a light that wraps a little past the shadow edge as marble's does, the metals with a tinted reflection; the page's brows and lashes leave the picture, and a hair colour of a stone follows the stone. The **Adjust** fold holds the stone's **Polish**, from rough to polished. A skin tone or an undertone makes the character skin again, and so does **Use own**. On a card with a baked skin a stone shows over it, and the tab says so. The look keeps the stone as `skin.stone` with its `skin.polish` (`anny-viewer/look@4`), and the card keeps them in its `look` block.

## The interface

The interface is React with [shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com) components (Radix primitives underneath) and Tailwind, in `viewer/src/shell/`, styled by the OpenSculptBoy Design System: white marble and nero marquina as two equal themes, kintsugi gold for the chosen thing and the one primary action, square-ish corners and a 4-pixel spacing scale. The 3D view is the renderer of `viewer/src/main.ts` with its single `<canvas>`, its frame loop and its GPU budget.

- **The design system.** `viewer/src/shell/design/` holds its tokens (`tokens.json`), its components (`osb.css`, one class per shadcn part), the Tailwind theme that maps shadcn's colour roles onto the tokens (`tailwind.css`) and the page's own rules (`page.css`). `build.mjs` writes the tokens as CSS variables for both themes, compiles Tailwind with the classes that `src/shell` uses (with `tailwindcss`'s own compiler, which has no native parts, so every machine builds the same page), and inlines the fonts: Cinzel, Instrument Sans and Instrument Serif (SIL Open Font License 1.1). The icons are Phosphor Icons in the Light weight (MIT licence), written into `viewer/src/ui/icon_paths.ts` by `node viewer/icons.mjs`. The page loads nothing from the network.
- **The components.** `viewer/src/shell/components/ui/` holds the shadcn/ui components that the page uses, copied in as source as shadcn intends: Button, Tabs, Toggle Group, Radio Group, Dropdown Menu, Tooltip, Dialog and Popover. The frame is `TopBar.tsx` (the brand, the character, the modes and the actions), `Panels.tsx` (the mode's panel, the tool panel, the rail and the phone bar), `Frame.tsx` (the view bar, the welcome card, the progress card and the Open dialog), `NewScreen.tsx` and `CharacterPanel.tsx`.
- **React and the frame loop.** React stays out of the renderer's frames. The store of `viewer/src/shell/state.ts` holds what the person chose (the mode, its section, the panels, the theme) and changes on clicks and keys only. The renderer's news (the look, the motion, the face, a tenth of a second) goes through the bus of the same file, and the components that follow the look while a slider moves (the sliders, the swatches, the character's name and dot) paint themselves through refs, so a drag renders nothing in React.
- **The sections.** Every section of the six modes is React (`viewer/src/shell/sections/`): Body, Face, Skin and eyes, Hair, Dress, Express, Pose, Sheet, Export and Settings, with the small controls of `components/controls.tsx` and the `Slider` component. The Sheet section draws the controls of `viewer/src/sheet/controller.ts`, which holds the settings, the build and the layout view. The Rig and Scene panels of the View menu are still the page's own DOM (`viewer/src/ui/rig_ui.ts`, `stage_ui.ts`), hosted by `DomSlot` with their ids, state and listeners. The slider keeps a native range input, as the design system's preview draws it, so the arrow keys work and the page tests keep their selectors.
- **Scripts and tests.** `window.__shell` gives the store and its actions (`__shell.actions.openMode('create', 'skin')`, `setTheme('dark')`, `showOpen()`). The ids that the page tests use stay: `#insp-left` (the mode's panel, with `data-mode`), `#insp-right` (the tool panel), `#sec-<id>`, `#tab-<mode>` and `#tab-<section>`, `#open-files` and `#open-folder`. The View menu's **Settings** item opens the Settings tool panel (`settingsSlot` in `viewer/src/shell/context.ts`).
- **Checks.** `test.test_viewer_frame` checks the store's actions in node (`viewer/test/frame.mjs`) and, in the built page (local only), the first visit's starter, the six modes, the welcome card, the theme, the figure framed between the bars, the files that a drop routes (GLB and VRM files with a card, a `.zip`), Save card and its card opened again, the New screen, the stone library, the Character panel, View › Settings, `Ctrl O`, the phone layout and `?shot`.

## Opening files

**Open** in the top bar, its drop zone, the New screen, the Dress mode's **Open files** and **Open folder**, and a drop anywhere on the page share one path (`viewer/src/shell/open.ts`). Each file goes to the loader of what it holds:

| Files | Where they go |
|---|---|
| A character card (`.json`) with its files, their folder or their `.zip` | the card loader of the Dress mode: Dress when the card has pieces, else Create |
| An OpenSculptBoy GLB or VRM file | the card in its scene extras: a card of sliders opens whole; a card that names files of its own opens with them (picked or dropped together), and else asks for its folder or `.zip`, with **Open the sliders alone** |
| Wearable packages (`.wearable.json`) without a card | loose pieces in Dress |
| Sheet settings (`opensculptboy/sheet-settings@1`) | the Sheet mode |
| A character of the page (`anny-viewer/look`) | Create |
| A picture (`.png`, `.jpg`, `.webp`) | Pose, posed from the picture |
| Another 3D model | a message that explains the fit, with its link |

A card that opens shows its steps in a progress card: reading the files, building the body, the skin's maps, the eyes, the mouth and the hair, and the dress. A file that the page cannot open shows a message in plain words in the Open dialog, with the next step as a button. A `.zip` may hold its entries stored or deflated; the page unpacks a deflated entry with the browser's `DecompressionStream`.

## Saving the character

**Save card** (Export, or `Ctrl S`) writes the character shown as a character card ([Character card](character-card.md)) of schema 5: the name, the sliders, the face shapes and the `look` block with the skin (or the stone and its polish), the hair and the eyes. For an open card it keeps the card's own fields (its detail layer, its material and its wardrobe) and writes the card with every file it was opened with as one `.zip`, with the card as `character.json` and the files at their paths beside it; a card without files of its own saves as `.json`. Both open again in the page, and the command line exports them (`opensculptboy export out.glb --character character.json`). `viewer/src/card/file.ts` holds the look block, the card, the `.zip` and the card of a GLB's extras without the DOM; `test.test_viewer_card_file` runs it in node (`viewer/test/card_file.mjs`) and reads the page's cards with `opensculptboy.Character`.

## Wearables

The **Dress** mode (the `D` key) dresses the character in the outfits of a character card ([Wearables](wearables.md)). **Open** in the top bar, or the mode's **Open files** and **Open folder**, picks the card and its wearable packages, and a drop of the files or their folder anywhere on the page does the same. The page reads the files in the browser: it sends them nowhere and keeps them only until it closes. The published page holds no wearables.

- The card's sliders, face shapes, local changes and detail layer shape the body, and its baked maps, eyes and parts draw it as its GLB export does ([A card's look](#a-cards-look)).
- The section lists the outfits and every piece with a switch. The page first wears the card's `active_outfit`, and no outfit where the card names none, as an export does. A piece of another outfit takes the slot of the worn outfit's piece, as `--show` does in an export. The hides rules hide groups of other pieces while their piece shows.
- **Hide covered skin** leaves the body triangles under the shown pieces out of the picture, as an export does by default.
- The pieces are meshes skinned by the page's rig, so they follow the poses and clips. A slider edit moves them with each new body (the bindings; the skin weights stay as they are); the push-out and the covered skin follow when the sliders rest for a quarter of a second. Their closest-point and line-partner queries run on worker threads (all cores but two, at most six; `?querypool=off` keeps them on the page's thread), and the rest runs in slices of about 8 ms with frames between them, so that the page keeps drawing, and the pieces show their push-out, the covered skin and the tangents of their normal maps for the new body together when the last slice ends. A choice in the section starts them at once.
- In the shadow maps a masked garment material casts the shadow of its fragments at or above its alpha cutoff, and a blended one the shadow of its fragments with an alpha of 0.5 or more. The page draws the shadow maps once for each change of the picture, so a blended material casts a cut shadow, while its own picture averages its transparency over the accumulated frames.
- A piece whose own weights or rigid bone name a mouth bone of the `anny-mouth` rig (`jaw`, `tongue00` to `tongue07.L/R`) moves it with `head`, as Anny's rig moves the skin weights of those bones, and the section notes it. A piece that fails to load or to dress (a missing file, a bone that the page's rig lacks) stays out with a message in the section and an error in the console; the other pieces dress.
- A rigid piece turns with its bone's skin: the page has the bone heads of every body and none of Anny's rest rotations, so the rotation comes from the vertices that the bone skins, turned about the bone's head as Anny's rig turns them (within 2 mm of Anny's on the test bodies).
- The dressing (`viewer/src/wearables/dress.ts`, `geometry.ts`) repeats `opensculptboy.wearables` without the DOM and three.js, so a worker can run it. `test.test_viewer_wearables` checks it against Python with synthetic outfits in node, and `test.test_viewer_wearables_page` checks the section in the built page (local only), with the files picked through the Open path's file input and dropped on the page.
- The skin triangles that garments bind to come from the page's body quads with the codes of `viewer/src/wearables/skin_split.ts`, written by `python -m opensculptboy.wearables.viewer`.

## A card's look

A card opened in the page shows as its GLB export shows it, with the page's lights. `viewer/src/card/data.ts` plans what the page takes from the card, and `viewer/src/card/look.ts` draws it.

- **Body.** The card's sliders, face shapes and local changes shape the coarse body, and its level-0 detail offsets move the coarse vertices. The page shows one level of the detail layer: the level of the texture bake, so that the normal maps match the surface they were baked on, or without a bake the finest level up to 2. The page's fine body is the mixed subdivision of Anny's body quads (three levels on the head and the neck, two elsewhere), and the page adds the level's offsets after its own level of the same number. `opensculptboy.viewer.detail_rows` maps each row of the page's levels 1 and 2 to the row of the card topology's full level with the same stencil.
- **Sculpt cards.** On a card of the `sculpt` topology the page hides the pocket of Anny's faces at the inner eye corner and draws the faces of the eye-corner patch there, at the card's level. The rows of the page's levels whose stencil the patch changed (about 1 % of them) move to the closest point of the card topology's level on the template, and take the blend of the offsets of that point's three rows. The skin's normals at the pocket's border come from the faces that show, and the patch's border takes the place and the normal of the skin there, so that the two meet. The pocket casts no shadow either.
- **Skin.** The skin samples the card's base colour, normal and roughness maps through the UVs of Anny's atlas, by the face-varying subdivision of the detail layer, with the head tile's maps and texture transform on the head's island. The fine vertices on the atlas's seams have one copy per side (`opensculptboy.viewer.seam_split`). The skin's roughness factor, specular, index of refraction, normal scale and subsurface weight and radius follow the GLB export's skin material ([Character card](character-card.md)), and the page's subsurface pass takes the subsurface share of the diffuse light. The page traces no second bounce of light, so the skin adds a share of the key light that the skin around reflects (`CARD_BOUNCE` in `viewer/src/card/look.ts`), more where the surface is occluded; without it the hollows under the brows and beside the nose turn black where the GLB's path-traced renders show lit skin. The page's own pores, fine lines, relief and skin colours stay out while the card's maps show.
- **Eyes.** A card with a baked eye layout gets the single-surface eyeball of `opensculptboy.eyes` on the spheres of its fitted eyeballs, with the iris radius read from the layout and the cornea's bulge, looking where the reference looks. The page keeps its own eyes for a card without a layout.
- **Parts.** The reference's lashes, inner mouth and teeth ride on the head bone with the GLB export's materials, and the page's own lashes and brows step aside for them. The page leaves out the generic teeth. The card's facial actions are the face that the page shows when the card opens ([Faces](#faces)).
- **Hair and brows.** The reference's hair and brows of a card (`material.hair`, `material.brows`, [Fit to a reference](reference-fit.md#hair-and-brows)) ride on the head bone, one mesh per groom material with the GLB export's material: its texture, base colour, alpha mode, cutoff and sides, and the hair's vertex colours. A blended material drops each fragment with the chance of its transparency, as the garments do, so the page needs no drawing order. The page's strand hair stays off for a card with reference hair and stays available for a card without it; the page's own brows step aside for the reference's brows and for a baked skin.
- **Blinks.** A card whose bake wrote its blinks (`material.reference_eyes.blinks`, `opensculptboy.eyes.page`) blinks as its GLB export does: the file holds the export's blink targets (the reference's closed eyes brought to meet), their in-between correctives and the squints, for the rows of the bake's level that they move and for the points of the lashes and the brows. The page carries them to its fine body through the subdivision that follows that level (sparse shapes of the moved vertices, with the change of their normals, applied as the soft-tissue correctives are), to the sculpt patch by its card rows, and to the lashes and the brows by their points, and weighs them as the GLB animation `eyeBlink.close` plays them. A timer closes both eyes over 0.3 s every 2.5 to 6 seconds (`?blink=off` and the shot mode leave it off). The **Eyes** block of the Dress mode's Wearables section switches the timer, blinks once, and sets the closure and the squint that the blinks start from.
- **Faces.** The card's own mouth poses and eye targets play as faces, and its parts follow the face ([Faces](#faces)).
- **Checks.** `test.test_viewer_card` checks the UVs and seams against the detail layer's level 2, the rows of both topologies, and in node the eye layout, the eye axes, the eyeball's surface, the level offsets inside the page's subdivision, the local changes against Anny, and the plan, the groom and blinks files and the weights of the blink targets (`viewer/test/groom_parity.mjs`).

## Faces

The page plays Anny's 52 ARKit facial actions as the GLB and VRM exports write them, on the page's coarse body, on its subdivided levels and on a card's detail layer. `viewer/src/face/` holds the faces: `api.ts` is the interface for the rest of the page (the Sheet mode, a later editor and scripts), `rig.ts` the maths, `morph.ts` the face on the GPU and `expressions.ts` the face of the page.

- **The Express mode** (the `X` key) holds the 14 VRM presets of the exports (`opensculptboy/data/vrm/expressions.json`: the five vowels, the three blinks, happy, angry, sad, relaxed, surprised and neutral) as buttons, the 10 visemes in a group that folds, and an intensity. A button fades the face over 0.18 s.
- **A face** is a mix of actions with weights from 0 to 1, a gaze (yaw to the figure's left and pitch up, in degrees) and a turn of the head (turn to the figure's left, tilt toward its left shoulder and nod down, in degrees), as `face/api.ts` gives it: `face().set(state)` shows a face and resolves once the page draws it, `face().preset(name, weight)` gives the mix of a preset or a viseme, and `face().get()` the face shown. Left and Right are the figure's own sides.
- **The data.** The viewer data build (`python -m anny.viewer build`) writes each action's target as sparse offsets of the coarse body (`face_action_ids` and `face_action_offsets`, about 49,000 offsets for the 52 actions), and the vertices of the face's block (`face_region`); the page's data grows by 0.5 MB, compressed, with the mouth cavity kept behind the skin as the exports keep it (`opensculptboy.viewer.face_tables`). The page carries each action to its fine body with its own subdivision, sparse from level to level, when the action first shows or while the page rests after its load.
- **The jaw.** `jawOpen` plays as the `anny-mouth` rig's jaw turn with its corrective, as the GLB animation `jawOpen.jaw` plays it ([The mouth](mouth.md)): at weight w the jaw turns by w times the turn of Anny's default body (about 22 degrees) about the jaw's head, carried with the head to the current body, and the corrective, computed for each body, takes the weight w, so that weight 1 shows Anny's `jawOpen` exactly. The jaw's weights are those of the `anny-mouth` rig, carried to the fine body by the subdivision.
- **The eyes.** A gaze turns the page's eyes and a card's eyes about their centres through Anny's eye-look actions, so that the lids follow, as far as the actions reach, and past that alone. The page's brows and lashes follow the skin under their roots.
- **The head.** A turn of the head turns the neck and the head bones in their own frames over the pose shown (`neck01` to `head` in shares of 0.3, 0.25, 0.2 and 0.25).
- **A card's faces.** An opened card adds its reference's mouth poses as the actions `mouth.<pose>` (`material.mouth.poses`, `opensculptboy/mouth-poses@1`: the offsets of level 0, the corrections of the level that the page shows and the inner mouth's own offsets), and the vowel presets play the poses `viseme_a` to `viseme_o` as the card's VRM export plays them. The card's eye targets (`material.reference_eyes.blinks`) play `eyeBlinkLeft`, `eyeBlinkRight`, `eyeSquintLeft` and `eyeSquintRight` in place of Anny's, with their in-between correctives, and the blink timer closes the eyes from the face's closure. The page plays a pose as one target, as the VRM files do; the GLB export also turns the jaw for it.
- **A card's parts.** The reference's lashes and brows follow the skin under them. The lower teeth, the lower gums, the floor of the mouth and the tongue follow the jaw (each point of the lower arch of `opensculptboy.teeth` and `opensculptboy.mouth.inner`) and the rigid motion of Anny's tongue in the other jaw actions, as the exports move them; in `tongueOut` the reference's tongue follows Anny's 4 nearest tongue vertices. A sculpt card's patch follows the coarse body's face.
- **On the GPU.** The fine shapes of the face shown live in two array textures of half floats, one texel per vertex of the face's block (the 229,000 vertices that any action or the jaw moves, which `build.mjs` puts together in the vertex order): the offsets in one, the change of the normals at weight 1 in the other, and in layer 0 the jaw's corrective and weights. A mix blends the normals' changes by its weights, as the normals of morph targets blend, so where two of its shapes move the same skin its normals differ from those of the moved triangles by a few degrees. The skin's vertex shaders (`FACE_GLSL`, the define `FACE_MORPH`) add the weighted layers of up to 16 shapes and turn the lower face with the jaw, in the picture and in the shadow maps. A change of the face costs a few uniforms and the positions of the few vertices that the brows, the lashes and a card's parts follow; a shape that shows for the first time costs the upload of its layer (about 3.7 MB). `?facegpu=off` plays the faces on the CPU (`FaceLayer` in `rig.ts`, with the normals of the moved triangles), as a page without the face's block in its data does.
- **Checks.** `test.test_viewer_expressions` checks the page's faces against Anny's in Python: the coarse body and the subdivided body for six mixes of actions with the jaw's turn, the jaw against `opensculptboy.mouth.jaw` and Anny's `jawOpen` at weight 1, the sparse subdivision against the dense one, the rest after a face, the eyes' turns and the tongue's motion. Its local-only classes check the faces on the built data (`viewer/test/face_body.mjs`) and in the built page: the interface, and the GPU's positions and normals against the CPU's.

## Sheet

The **Sheet** mode (the `S` key) makes a character sheet of the figure shown: one page with its turnaround, its expressions and its poses, to share or to print. The page shows the character as the page draws it, with a card's look and its worn outfit when a card is open.

- The mode opens a layout view beside its panel, in place of the 3D view: the page as it will print. The panels fill in one by one while the sheet builds, with a progress bar, and **Stop** ends a build. The close button of the layout view hides it.
- **Build sheet** renders the pictures, and **Download PNG** writes the page (about 4096 by 6000 pixels at the Print quality). A change of the name, the title or the background redraws the page without a new build.
- Each panel has a switch, and a panel without pictures stays out:
  - **Info block**: the name (the card's or the look's by default) and a title.
  - **Turnaround**: the full figure from the front, three-quarter, side and back, at one scale on one ground line. Its pose is *Relaxed* by default.
  - **Expressions**: a grid of faces, by default neutral, happy, angry, sad, relaxed, surprised, a blink and the five vowels, in the turnaround's pose. The **Face** framing (the default) takes each cell from just below the chin to just above the top of the head or its hair, so the face fills about 60 % of the cell, and turns the figure so the face looks straight at the camera; tall hair and headgear are cut a sixth of the head's height above its top, so the face keeps its share on every character. **Head and shoulders** takes the shoulders and the top of the chest as well. The faces come from the page's face interface (`viewer/src/face/api.ts`); while no face driver is registered, every cell shows the neutral face.
  - **Poses**: six poses of the library that show a standing character's manner (*Weight on one leg*, *Arms crossed*, *Hands on hips*, *Thinking*, *Waving goodbye*, *Hero stance*), at one scale on one ground line. A pose slot opens the pose library or **From a picture**; up to eight slots.
- **Style** picks the look of the pictures. The page has the **Textured** style: the page's own look. The list in `viewer/src/sheet/styles.ts` is where other styles join. **Background** puts the page on a warm mid-dark grey (the default), on light paper for print, on a dark ground, or on a transparent one; the name, the title, the labels and the lines take the colours of their ground (`COLOURS` in `viewer/src/sheet/compose.ts`, with a contrast ratio of at least 7 for the name, 4.5 for the labels and 3 for the faint texts, checked in node), and a transparent page keeps the paper's dark text. **Quality** is **Print** (pictures at print size, 24 samples each) or **Draft** (half the size, 8 samples).
- **Lights** are **Studio** by default: the sheet's own lights (`SHEET_LIGHTS` in `viewer/src/main.ts`), the same for every character and every view, with a soft key from the front on the figure's right and above, a large dim fill on its left, two strip lights behind for the rims on both edges, and a rim light from behind that separates the figure from the page. Their exposure comes from a meter: before the pictures, the build renders the whole figure from the front in the turnaround's pose and takes the largest exposure at which at most 0.2 % of the figure's pixels have a channel at 250 of 255 or more, the 95th percentile of their display luminance stays at or below 0.74, and the median at or below 0.58 (`SHEET_METER`). The page's display pass tops out near 0.86 with its AgX curve, and above about 0.75 the curve's shoulder flattens pale skin, hair and cloth, so the meter keeps the highlights below it; the whole sheet takes that exposure. **Exposure (stops)** moves it up or down. **The page's** lights take the lighting preset and the exposure of the Scene panel (View menu) instead. The page's own view keeps its preset; the sheet's lights replace it only while a sheet builds. The page's **Studio** preset had the same fault: at its old exposure of 0.88 the default character's figure had a median display luminance of 0.80 and a 95th percentile of 0.84, in the shoulder, so its exposure is now 0.45 (0.69 and 0.74); the Scene panel's exposure slider still moves it.
- Each picture renders offscreen with a camera of its own: a long lens (8°) in front of the figure, looking along the horizontal, framed from a small probe picture of the whole figure (its hair, its outfit and a raised hand included), and the figure turns on its vertical axis, so the lights fall on every view alike. The pictures go through the page's own display pass (its AgX tone mapping, saturation and contrast) at the sheet's exposure. The turnaround's views are 2048 pixels tall, the poses 1536 and the expression cells 960 (768 or 864 wide), each with a transparent background. The accumulation keeps the coverage as alpha, so the edges of the hair and the outfit blend into any background.
- A build holds the scene: the hair's physics, the blink timer and a playing clip stop, the 3D view keeps its last picture, dimmed, so the figure does not jump through the sheet's poses, and the figure's pose and turn come back afterwards. Each picture draws in passes of about two million pixels per frame, so the page keeps drawing; on a desktop GPU a sheet of the default character builds in about 5 seconds.
- The section keeps its settings in one plain object (`viewer/src/sheet/settings.ts`, format `opensculptboy/sheet-settings@1`): the style, the background, the name and the title, each panel with its switch, its pose slots (a library name, or the bone rotations of a pose from a picture), its view, its framing and its expressions, and the render scale, samples, lights and exposure stops. The page remembers it in the browser; a background that the user never picked follows the default.
- Scripts use the same build: `window.sheet.build(settings)` resolves to the PNG bytes of the page (`window.sheet.base64(bytes)` gives them as text), `window.sheet.report()` gives the page's size, the build time, the lights and the meter's reading (the exposure, the limit that set it, and the clipped share and luminance percentiles of the figure), and each picture's size and coverage, `window.sheet.picture(panel, index)` one picture as a PNG with its transparent background, and `window.sheet.defaults()` and `window.sheet.normalise(settings)` the settings object.
- `viewer/src/sheet/layout.ts` holds the framing and the page layout without the DOM. `test.test_viewer_sheet` checks them and the settings in node (`viewer/test/sheet.mjs`), and builds a sheet of the default character in the built page (local only).

## AI providers

View › Settings holds the **AI providers** panel (`viewer/src/shell/sections/SettingsSection.tsx`). It keeps API keys of Anthropic, OpenAI and OpenRouter on this device, encrypted, and unlocks them with a passkey: Windows Hello, Touch ID, a phone or a security key. The page needs no helper program. The logic lives in `viewer/src/ai/` without a framework, so any interface can use it: `vault.ts` (the key vault), `session.ts` (keys for one visit), `crypto.ts`, `passkey.ts` (WebAuthn with the PRF extension), `store.ts` (IndexedDB) and `providers.ts` (the provider adapters). `test.test_viewer_ai` checks it: in CI, the vault and the adapters in node with a fake passkey and fake providers (`viewer/test/ai_vault.mjs`, `TestVaultNode`) and the page's server (`TestServe`); locally, the built page with Chromium's virtual authenticator, where the test routes every request of the page and fails on any request to a host other than the page and the provider of the key it carries (`TestAiPage`).

### Where the page must be open

For now the panel works on the page served at `http://localhost` alone; a hosted copy is a later choice. Passkeys need a secure page with a domain name, which `http://localhost` is. Chromium refuses passkeys to a page opened as a file, and it gives every page opened as a file one shared storage, so the panel stays off on such a page and shows the way instead: in the page's folder run `python serve_viewer.py --open`, which opens `http://localhost:41730/anny_viewer.html`. `serve_viewer.py` and `serve.json` come with the page in `viewer/dist/`, and the script needs Python 3.8 or later and nothing else. It serves the page file alone, plus MediaPipe's files beside it, the data files of a page in parts and the starters (`starters/<statue>.zip` with its `.png`, and `<statue>.light.json`), and answers every other path with 404, so no other file of the folder shares the page's origin. It listens on both loopback addresses, `127.0.0.1` and `::1`, so no other program can take the page's address on the second one, and it stops with a message when either is taken (on Windows it asks for the port alone, `SO_EXCLUSIVEADDRUSE`). It answers only requests that name `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]` at its port, and it keeps the page out of frames and caches. The address must say `localhost`, because passkeys refuse an address in numbers. The vault belongs to the page's origin, port included, so the port is fixed: 41730, in `viewer/src/ai/serve.json`, which the page and the script both read. IANA lists 41730 as unassigned, and it is no common tool's default (8765, the port before, is AnkiConnect's). Another port opens an empty vault. The **Open folder** button and a dropped card folder work on a served page as on a file page.

### What the page loads

Nothing from another host. The page's scripts, its fonts (Instrument Sans and Instrument Serif from `@fontsource`, OFL-1.1), MediaPipe's task code (`@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.0.1, bundled) and the gzip fallback `fflate` are inside the page, which is 31.3 MB. MediaPipe's WebAssembly loader and binary and its pose and hand models (29.3 MB together) are files in `viewer/dist/mediapipe/`, in git, because they change only when the pinned MediaPipe version changes; `build.mjs` pins each by SHA-256, takes the WebAssembly from the npm package and the models from Google's model store (or the copies already there), and refuses any file with other bytes. The page fetches each file from its own origin with its hash as the request's integrity, so the browser refuses other bytes, and runs the loader from a script element with the same integrity. A Content Security Policy in `viewer/shell.html` lets only the page's own scripts and MediaPipe's loader run, by their SHA-256 hashes (which `build.mjs` writes), lets WebAssembly compile and the page's workers start from blob URLs, and lets the page connect only to itself, to blob URLs and to `https://api.anthropic.com`, `https://api.openai.com` and `https://openrouter.ai`. A browser that checks only inline scripts by hash runs the loader from a blob URL of the bytes it checked on fetch. MediaPipe's task code sends usage records (the task, the platform and timings) to `odml.pa.googleapis.com` every minute; `build.mjs` cuts that call out of the bundle, and the policy would block it as well (the browser test found it). A policy in a page cannot stop a top-level navigation, and it does not restrain code that already runs in the page; it limits where injected code could send a key.

### What is stored where

- IndexedDB of the page's origin, database `opensculptboy-ai`: for each passkey its credential ID, a random 32-byte PRF salt and the data key wrapped with AES-GCM under that passkey's key; for each provider the API key sealed with AES-GCM (a random 12-byte nonce, and additional data that names the provider, so that a record moved to another provider does not decrypt), the date it was added and the result of its last check.
- The authenticator: the passkey and its secret. Only the authenticator can compute the PRF output, and only after the person confirms with a fingerprint, a face, a PIN or a touch of the key.
- Memory, while the vault is unlocked: the data key as a non-extractable WebCrypto key, whose bytes no script can read out; any script in the page can still use it while the vault is open (see below). The vault locks on **Lock**, and when the page closes or goes to the back-forward cache; it has no idle lock. An API key is decrypted for one call and handed to the adapter. JavaScript cannot erase a string, so that copy lasts until the garbage collector frees it.
- The character card, the exports, localStorage, the URL and the console never get a key. The key field is a password field outside any form, with spell checking off, and it empties as soon as the key is stored.

### The passkey's role

Setup creates a passkey with the PRF extension and a random salt. Unlock asks the passkey for its PRF output with that salt and with user verification. HKDF-SHA-256 turns the output into the key-wrapping key, which unwraps the data key; the page then drops the output and the wrapping key. No server checks the passkey's signature: the protection comes from the PRF output, which only the authenticator can give. A second passkey (`KeyVault.addPasskey`) wraps the same data key, so either passkey opens the vault. **Forget** removes one key's record and needs no unlock. **Forget all** removes every record and asks the authenticator to drop the passkey, where the browser has `PublicKeyCredential.signalUnknownCredential`. An authenticator without PRF cannot make the key, so setup stores nothing and the panel switches to keys for this visit (`viewer/src/ai/session.ts`), with a plain line that says so: a pasted key stays in memory for the visit, serves its checks and calls, and is gone when the page closes or reloads. The page remembers only that this browser's passkey had no PRF (a flag in the interface's saved state), and **Try a passkey again** leaves that mode. A lost passkey loses the stored keys; the provider's dashboard makes new ones.

PRF support in 2026: Windows Hello on Windows 11 with the February 2026 update and Chrome or Edge 147 or later, or Firefox 148 or later; Windows 10 has none. Safari 18 or later with iCloud Keychain on macOS 15 and iOS 18. Chrome with Google Password Manager passkeys on every platform. Security keys with `hmac-secret` in Chrome, Edge and Firefox. Password-manager extensions vary.

### What an attacker can read

- With a copy of the browser profile: the ciphertexts, the salts and the credential IDs. Without the authenticator and the person's confirmation, these give no key. The passkey's secret stays in the authenticator (a TPM for Windows Hello, the Secure Enclave on Apple devices).
- With code running in the page at any time during a visit, such as an injected script or a browser extension that may read the page: the decrypted keys, at once while the vault is unlocked, and at the next unlock or use while it is locked, because the page itself must use the keys. The page loads no code from another host (above), and its policy limits where such code could send a key.
- Another page on `localhost`, at any port, shares the passkey's relying party ID `localhost`, so it can ask for the same passkey, and the authenticator shows its prompt. The salt is not secret by design: the protection is the browser's isolation of this origin's database, which another port cannot read. Code that has the salt (for example from a copy of the profile) and a page on `localhost` gets the right output from one confirmed prompt. Another `localhost` page can also ask the browser to delete the passkey (`signalUnknownCredential`), which loses the stored keys.
- Local programs on the computer are out of scope for now (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29). A program of any account that serves its own page at `http://localhost:41730` while the person's server is down owns the vault's origin: it reads the records and can ask for the genuine passkey. `serve_viewer.py` holds both loopback addresses while it runs, and a hosted copy at an HTTPS origin is the lasting answer. A program with control of the person's account can do what code in the page can.

### Provider calls

Each call goes from the browser straight to the provider, with no cookies and no referrer: `api.anthropic.com` with the header `anthropic-dangerous-direct-browser-access: true`, `api.openai.com` and `openrouter.ai`. Their answers to a browser's preflight allow any origin (measured on 2026-10-29). The key check reads the model list (Anthropic and OpenAI) or the key's own record (OpenRouter, whose model list needs no key). `complete(key, messages)` sends one chat and returns its text; errors come out in plain words, with anything that looks like a key taken out of the provider's message.

## Frame budget

A frame of the page draws the shadow maps, the scene, the skin's subsurface pass and one sample of the accumulated picture. The skin's fragments cost the most at a close view of the face, where the body fills the screen.

- While the figure or the camera moves, the page draws at most 60 frames per second (`window.setFpsCap(n)` sets another limit, 0 none), and a controller keeps the GPU time of a frame within a budget of 6 ms (`?budget=N` sets another, `?budget=off` none). The page measures each frame with `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` where the browser has it. Over the budget, or with late frames, the controller lowers the render scale and the share of hair strands in steps; with room to spare for two seconds, it raises them again.
- The hair draws a share of its strands that follows the area of the head on the screen and the controller's step.
- Once everything rests, the page refines the picture at full resolution with every strand, and it stops when a new sample no longer changes the picture: from the eighth sample on, every other sample measures the mean change of the picture on a grid of points, and two readings below 0.001 in a row end the refinement (`?converge=0` refines to the full count of samples). The page then draws nothing until something changes, and hidden hair runs no physics.
- `window.gpuProfile()` gives the GPU time of each stage of a frame, `window.convergence()` the state of the refinement, and the Performance card both.

## Building the page

The build needs node 22 or later:

```bash
uv sync --extra viewer
uv run python -m anny.viewer build      # the model data and the page
node viewer/build.mjs --reuse-data      # the page alone, after a change to viewer/src
node viewer/build.mjs --parts <dir>     # the model data in separate files, for hosts that limit the size of a file
```
