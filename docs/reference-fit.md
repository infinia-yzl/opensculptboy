# Fitting a reference mesh

`opensculptboy fit` reproduces a given humanoid figure with anny: it fits anny's parameters and pose to a target mesh of any topology, and it keeps what the parametric body cannot reach as a detail layer of per-vertex offsets on subdivisions of anny's mesh. The first target is a replica of a Dragon's Dogma 2 character, given as an appearance handoff folder (the format described below), but any skinned or static triangle mesh in metres works.

```bash
uv sync --extra viewer                                       # SciPy for the fit, embreex for the texture bake
opensculptboy fit handoff/ --out character.json --detail 1      # the card, character.detail0.safetensors and character.detail1.safetensors
opensculptboy fit handoff/ --out character.json --textures      # also character.base_color.png, character.normal.png and character.eyes.png
opensculptboy export character.glb --character character.json --detail 1
opensculptboy export character.vrm --character character.json --detail 1 --author "Your name"
```

```python
from opensculptboy.fit import fit_handoff

result = fit_handoff("handoff/", detail=1)
print(result.errors[1]["reverse"]["mean"])   # mean distance from the target to the fitted surface, in mm
result.write("character.json")
```

## The handoff folder

The folder holds these files:

```
handoff/
    replica.glb        the skinned parts in their edited bind pose; metres, Y up, glTF axes (the figure faces +Z)
    textures/*.png     the textures of the parts
    appearance.json    {"landmarks": {name: [x, y, z]}, "parts": ..., "materials": ...}
```

`opensculptboy.fit.handoff.load_handoff` reads every primitive of `replica.glb` as one part, with its node transforms (the skinning is not applied, since the parts are in their bind pose), and turns it into Anny's frame: metres, Z up, the figure facing -Y, so that glTF's (x, y, z) becomes (x, -z, y). Each part keeps the name of its mesh, the name of its material, its UVs and its base colour and normal textures where the file has them.

`parts` may be a list of objects or an object keyed by part name, as the contract writes it (`{"body": {"node_name": ...}, "hair": {...}}`). Each entry names a mesh or node of the GLB file (`"name"`, `"mesh"`, `"node"` or `"node_name"`; a name also matches the nodes that start with it and an underscore, as some exporters split a part into `<node_name>_<suffix>` nodes) and may give a `"role"` (or `"kind"`, `"type"`, `"category"`); an entry of an object without one takes its key as the role. Entries of `materials` may give a `"role"` per material. The role of a primitive is, in this order: its material's declared role; the role that its material's name names; its part's declared role; the role that its own name names; else skin. The names name the roles `lashes`, `brows`, `beard`, `hair`, `mouth` (teeth, the tongue, the gums), `eyes`, `nails` and `clothing`, so that the eyes, the lashes and the inside of the mouth of a head part leave the skin even when the part itself is skin. The roles `skin`, `body`, `head`, `face`, `hands` and `feet` are skin, and only the skin takes part in the fit. The parts of role `eyes` give the eyeballs: each one that is a sphere (`opensculptboy.fit.reference.eye_spheres`) places anny's eyeball, and the texture bake takes the iris from them.

### Landmarks

The landmarks are optional. Each name of the table matches loosely (`LeftEyeOuter`, `eye.outer.L` and `left_eye_outer` all mean `eye_outer_l`), and `l` and `r` are the figure's own left and right (+X and -X in both frames). Unknown names are ignored.

| Landmark | Counterpart on anny | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| `eye_outer_l`, `eye_outer_r` | exocanthion (craniofacial landmark `ex`) | 1 |
| `eye_inner_l`, `eye_inner_r` | endocanthion (`en`) | 1 |
| `nose_tip` | pronasale (`prn`) | 1 |
| `mouth_corner_l`, `mouth_corner_r` | cheilion (`ch`) | 1 |
| `chin` | gnathion (`gn`) | 1 |
| `ear_l`, `ear_r` | tragion (`t`) | 0.5 |
| `ear_top_l`, `ear_top_r` | superaurale (`sa`), the highest point of the ear: the tip of a pointed ear | 0.2 |
| `ear_lobe_l`, `ear_lobe_r` | subaurale (`sba`), the lowest point of the lobe | 0.2 |
| `ear_back_l`, `ear_back_r` | postaurale (`pa`), the hindmost point of the ear | 0.2 |
| `ear_lateral_l`, `ear_lateral_r` | the point of the ear furthest from the head (`ela`) | 0.2 |
| `crown` | vertex (`v`), the top of the head | 0.5 |
| `neck_base` | the head of bone `neck01` | 0 |
| `shoulder_l`, `shoulder_r` | the head of `upperarm01` | 0.1 |
| `elbow_l`, `elbow_r` | the head of `lowerarm01` | 0.2 |
| `wrist_l`, `wrist_r` | the head of `wrist` | 0.2 |
| `hip_l`, `hip_r` | the head of `upperleg01` | 0 |
| `knee_l`, `knee_r` | the head of `lowerleg01` | 0.2 |
| `ankle_l`, `ankle_r` | the head of `foot` | 0.2 |

The face landmarks are points of the skin, and the body landmarks are joint centres, which sit differently in every rig; the weights reflect that. The neck base and the hips take no weight: they are read and reported, but the joint centres of the first real source lay 20 to 27 mm from anny's, and they pulled the placement and the pose further than they helped. A source may give a landmark twice, as a joint position and as the nearest point of the skin, the latter named with a `skin` or `surface` word (`mouth_corner_l` and `mouth_corner_l_skin`): the face landmarks take the point of the skin, and the joint centres the other. Loose names of the ear outline include `ear_tip_l` and `helix_tip_l` (`ear_top_l`), `earlobe_l` (`ear_lobe_l`) and `postaurale_l` (`ear_back_l`). Without landmarks the fit places the target by its bounding geometry: the highest point (the crown), the points furthest to each side (the fingertips) and the soles, which the same rule finds on anny. This assumes the contract's axes, with the figure standing upright and facing +Z.

### Proposed additions to the handoff contract

The tragion alone leaves the shape of the ear to the surface terms, and a pointed ear far from anny's then fits with crumpled folds. The fit reads four more points per ear, which a handoff can take from the head skin as the extremes of the outer ear's vertices (in glTF's frame with the figure facing +Z):

| Name | Point of the outer ear |
|---|---|
| `ear_top_l`, `ear_top_r` | the highest vertex (+Y): the tip of a pointed ear |
| `ear_lobe_l`, `ear_lobe_r` | the lowest vertex (-Y): the bottom of the lobe |
| `ear_back_l`, `ear_back_r` | the hindmost vertex (-Z) |
| `ear_lateral_l`, `ear_lateral_r` | the vertex furthest from the midline (largest \|X\|) |

Each is a point of the skin, so no `_skin` variant is needed. The outer ear is the handoff's optional `masks.ear` field: one entry per side, naming the part's mesh or node, its primitive or material, the `side` (`l` or `r`) and the indices of the outer ear's `vertices` in that primitive, without the line where the ear attaches to the head; the entry may also say where the mask comes from (`source`) and give that attachment line (`attachment`). `load_handoff` reads it onto the part (`HandoffPart.ear_vertices`) and ignores entries that name no loaded part. The real fit below takes the outline from such a mask by exactly these rules.

A standing ear also needs its mask: the vertices of the outer ear, and of them those along the line where the ear meets the head, its attachment. `masks.ear` of appearance.json holds one entry per skin part that has an ear, with the indices into that part's vertices:

```json
"masks": {"ear": [{"mesh": "head", "vertices": [812, 813, 860], "attachment": [812, 860]}]}
```

`"mesh"` (or `"node"`, `"part"`) names the part as `parts` does. A source can take `vertices` from an ear mask of the head part and `attachment` as the mask's vertices that share an edge with a vertex outside it; when `attachment` is missing, the fit takes that border itself (`opensculptboy.fit.register.mask_border`). `Reference.from_handoff` keeps them as the regions `"ear"` and `"ear_attachment"`. With the mask, the ears fit apart from the head (see the pipeline), and the measures of the ears use it.

## The pipeline

1. **Placement.** With landmarks, a rigid alignment of anny's landmarks onto the target's (Umeyama 1991); without them, the centres of the bounding landmarks. The height phenotype starts at the value whose stature matches the target.
2. **Parameters.** Adam over the 6 phenotypes, the local changes, the 113 face shapes, the turns of every bone but the root and the eyes, and a rigid placement, in 10 rounds of 30 steps. Each face shape sums the left and right MakeHuman targets of a pair of local changes (`chin-prominent` moves the vertices of `chin-prominent-incr` and `chin-prominent-decr`), so fitted together the two would split one movement between two controls and could run past the slider ranges. The fit therefore takes the face shapes on the 125 local changes of the face that share a target with one (`FitOptions.shared_targets`, `"face_shapes"`); those local changes stay at 0, the 131 local changes of the body stay free, and the asymmetry that the symmetric face shapes cannot hold is left to the detail layer. `"local_changes"` fits the local changes instead, and `"both"` both. Each round finds the closest points again. The loss adds point-to-plane distances from anny's vertices to the target surface, point-to-plane distances from a sample of 20,000 target vertices to anny's nearest vertices, the landmark distances and small ridge penalties on the local changes, the face shapes and the bone turns. Pairs farther apart than a limit (50 mm in the first round, 10 mm in the last) or whose normals disagree drop out. The first three rounds free the placement, the phenotypes and the large joints alone, and the fingers stay at rest until round 7 (`FitOptions.finger_rounds`), when the limit is under 20 mm: freed earlier, a finger of anny can slide onto its neighbour and stay there.
3. **Non-rigid refinement.** Anny's whole mesh moves onto the target by a non-rigid ICP in the spirit of optimal-step NICP (Amberg et al., CVPR 2007): closest points pull the vertices, and a Laplacian term keeps the displacement smooth, with a stiffness that falls over four stages. A reverse term pulls each vertex that is the nearest of some target vertices (within the distance limit, with agreeing normals) to their mean: closest points alone never pull a surface past its border, so without it the parts of a target that reach beyond anny's, such as the tip of a pointed ear or eyelids that cover more of the eye, stay uncovered. The eye sockets, the inside of the mouth and every closed part apart from the body (the eyeballs and the tongue) take no data, and neither does the lips' inner margin, the ring of vertices along the inside of the mouth (`FitOptions.lip_margin_weight`), which follows its neighbours: pulled onto a closed mouth, it folded into the corners as a notch. The sockets take the displacement of the nearest fitted skin in space, and a vertex of them still in front of the skin goes back behind it: along the mesh they hang from the lids' inner margins alone. The inside of the mouth and the lips' inner margin take the harmonic extension of the offsets of the skin around them in the rest frame (`smooth_mouth`, `FitOptions.mouth_lining`), so that they move with the lips as one smooth sheet: moved with the nearest skin in space, as the sockets are, the lining of the upper lip took the lower lip's displacement where the closed lips meet, and the lining of the lower lip the upper lip's, and the mouth's own shapes (`jawOpen` and the others act on the rest mesh) opened those folds into spikes. Last, `repair_mouth` smooths in the rest frame each edge of the mouth that folds by more than 60 degrees and by 45 more than on the card without its offsets, with a ring of its neighbours, within 5 faces of the inside of the mouth (the lips' hidden roll; `FitOptions.mouth_repair_rounds`, `mouth_repair_reach`, `mouth_repair_rings`). The target cannot judge these folds, since its closed lips meet in a crease of their own; anny's parametric mouth folds only where its lips meet. Before the repair, the vertices of the two lips' inner rolls that are each other's nearest vertex of the other lip within 1.5 mm on the parametric mesh (the lips told apart by the displacement of anny's `jawOpen` face unit, `jaw_share`) meet at the mean of their fitted positions (`lip_contact`, `stitch_lips`, `FitOptions.lip_contact`), and the repair holds them: the visible lips follow the target down to its crease and the roll behind it takes no data, so that the fitted lips part by a slit through which the inside of the mouth shows; the lining that followed the nearest skin used to plug that slit. `metrics.mouth_tears` counts the tearing edges of a card with the mouth closed and opened. Each eyeball moves and scales onto the target's eyeball sphere when the target has one, and otherwise moves with the skin around it, as the tongue does. The outer ears (`ear_weights`: the displacement of MakeHuman's ear translation targets, 1 on the ear and falling to 0 around it) are 3 times stiffer than the rest (`FitOptions.ear_stiffness`), and after the refinement their vertices relax along the target for 10 rounds (`relax_along`: each moves by half its umbrella vector within its tangent plane, then onto its closest point of the target), which spreads the stretch of a pointed tip, and of the texture on it, over the whole ear and keeps the folds from crumpling. Last, every vertex within 2 mm of the target whose normal agrees with it to 0.8 moves onto its closest point (`project_onto`, `FitOptions.project_distance`), but on the ears: the Laplacian term left the coarse vertices of level 0 off the surface by a few tenths of a millimetre, which showed as small lumps along the jaw and the cheeks. The forward errors are then close to 0 by construction, and the reverse errors measure the fit.
4. **Ears that stand off from the head.** With `masks.ear`, the ears fit apart from the head (`EarSplit`, `FitOptions.ear_split`). Anny's outer ears proper are `pinna`: of the vertices that the ear translation targets move in full, which also hold a strip of the head's skin around the root, those more than 2 mm off a quadratic fitted to the head's skin around the ear, with the hole they enclose (the concha). The reference splits into its ears (the triangles of the mask) and the rest, and in both stages and both directions anny's outer ears match the reference's ears alone and the rest of anny the rest: without the split, the reverse term pulled the head's skin behind a standing ear up to the back of the tip, and the skin stretched across the gap where the reference has none, while the tip itself stayed uncovered. A pair whose closest point lies on the cut, with an offset that runs along the surface, drops out (as Amberg et al. drop the pairs on the borders of a target): the vertices past the cut would all clamp onto it, and on level 2 they creased the skin at the front of the helix root. The root of anny's ear takes the attachment line instead, both ways (`FitOptions.ear_attachment_weight`): each root vertex toward its closest point of the line, and each point of the line pulls the nearest root vertex, so that the root covers the line to its ends. The vertices of either part along the cut take no part in the reverse term: the head's pulled the skin beside the root onto the line next to the root, and the two creased there. The cut is found on the welded reference (`welded_ids`): a game writes its ear as a piece of its own, stitched to the head by coincident vertices, so that the attachment line is a border of the file's mesh, and the cut, and with it the dropped pairs, would be empty. On each detail level, the mask of anny's outer ears moves within three rings of its border (six on level 2) to where the mesh crosses the attachment line (`relabel_ears`, `FitOptions.ear_relabel_rings`): a vertex of the band is ear when the ear part is nearer to it than the head part. `pinna` cuts anny's ear where it stands off from the head, and a mask may hold more of the flat skin around the root, as the front of the real replica's ear does (the tragus and the helix root, which meet the cheek with no step): there anny's head skin lay on the reference's ear, could match only the head beside it, slid off the ear onto the head and folded into a crack along the front of the ear. After the refinement, `fair_region` fairs the ears and two rings of the head's skin around them (four on level 1, eight on level 2) on the reference: five rounds of closest points and the reverse term with the square of the mean-value Laplacian of the positions, which is small on a smooth surface and large on a crumple, a fold or a spike, while the closest points let the vertices slide within the reference. `repair_folds` then fairs again, ten times as strongly, around each vertex that `fold_defects` finds: one folded by more than 60 degrees and by 45 more than the reference within 3 mm of its closest point, or whose normal turns away from the reference's where the reference does not fold. The ears are as stiff as the rest (`ear_split_stiffness`), the tangential relaxation of the ears is left out, and the skin around them takes the projection onto the head alone. Without `masks.ear` the fit runs as before, bit for bit.
5. **Lids.** With the target's eyeballs, `seat_lids` places the lids and the inner corner of each eye on what the target shows there (`FitOptions.seat_lids`): the fitted skin within 1.35 eyeball radii of the centre and 100 degrees of the gaze, and the socket's lining (and on `sculpt` the eye-corner patch) within 1.6, take the target's skin where that lies outside its eyeball's sphere and the sphere (at 0.99 of its radius, under the exported eyeball) where it goes under, with the reverse term, so that the target's tissue pulls the skin over the eyeball as far as it reaches, and a strong bi-Laplacian fairing; what already lies under the sphere stays, and skin within 0.23 mm over it (the exported cornea's shell and the lids' clearance) leaves that band, so that the exported eyeball keeps its size. On level 2 (`FitOptions.lid_clearance_rounds`), each vertex that enters the band takes a side once (out to its top, or under the eyeball) and the faired solve draws it there, so that its neighbours follow: moved radially on its own after the solve, as levels 0 and 1 still do, neighbouring vertices of the lid's edge that fell on either side of the band's middle stepped by the band's width, which showed on level 2 of the real replica as a jagged step in the lower lid's edge at the inner corner. On levels 0 and 1 an edge of the lid spans the band several times over and no step shows, and on the synthetic target of `TestCornerFit` the faired version turned more of the corner's faces over there (19 of 252 on level 1, against 16). On level 2 the band also leaves alone the skin that faces the eyeball (`FitOptions.lid_clearance_inward`), and the lids follow the target's skin where it goes inside its eyeball's sphere (`FitOptions.lid_follow_inside`). The exported eyes count only the skin that faces out from the eyeball (`eyes.geometry.bulge_clearance`), and the real replica's lower lid faces its eyeball where it goes under it. Moved under the eyeball by the band, that skin stood a ring away from the skin just outside the band, at 1.02 to 1.05 eyeball radii, which showed from below as a crease where the lower lid meets the inner corner, and 3.3 % of the corner's faces turned away from the replica's. The refinement leaves the sockets to `follow_skin`, which only moves them with the skin: the lids ended in a lip, and the corner in a trench open to the front, outside the eyeball, which rendered as a dark line (docs/sculpt-topology.md, "The eye corner in the fit").
6. **Detail levels.** Each level of the detail layer repeats the refinement on its subdivided mesh, starting from the level below, with a lower stiffness.
7. **Residuals.** The registered mesh of each level goes back to the rest frame by the inverse of each vertex's blended bone transform, and the offsets are the registered rest mesh minus the parametric rest mesh. Added to the subdivided rest mesh and skinned again, they give back the registered mesh.

`opensculptboy fit --topology sculpt` (`FitOptions.topology`) fits the `sculpt` mesh instead of anny's (docs/sculpt-topology.md): anny's mesh with patches at the inner corner of the eye and the root of the outer ear. The card and its offsets then take `sculpt`, a new vertex of a patch takes the region and the mask of its strongest anchor (or its role in `register.PATCH_ROLES`; the eye-corner patch is placed at the blends of its anchors, between the fitted lids and the eyeball, by `register.PatchAnchors`, and then on the target's tissue and eyeball by `register.seat_lids`), and with the ear-root patch the outer ear of the ear split is the part that the patch's root line cuts off the head, so that the root of the ear meets the reference's attachment line along that line. `opensculptboy convert` carries a fitted card and its offsets between both meshes.

The card holds the fitted parameters, the fitted pose (the target's bind pose, which the GLB export plays as the animation named `pose`), and the detail layer with the placement and the measured errors.

The code lives in `src/opensculptboy/fit/`: `handoff.py` (the folder), `reference.py` (the target, its landmarks and the closest-point queries), `register.py` (stages 1 to 6, `fit_reference`, `fit_levels` for the levels of a fitted card, `FitResult.write`; the ears in `pinna`, `EarSplit`, `fair_region` and `repair_folds`; the lids in `lid_region` and `seat_lids`), `metrics.py` (the measures below) and `residual.py` (the unposing, the offsets and `surface_error`). It reuses the Umeyama alignment and the uniform Laplacian of `anny.faces.authoring.nicp` and the closest-triangle search of `anny.hair`.

## The detail layer

A card with a detail layer uses character schema 2; a card without one is still written as schema 1, byte for byte as before, and both schemas load.

```json
{
  "schema": 2,
  "name": "character",
  "phenotype": {"gender": 0.62, "age": 0.48, "...": 0.5},
  "detail": {
    "levels": [
      {"subdiv": 0, "offsets": "character.detail0.safetensors", "error_mm": 0.26},
      {"subdiv": 1, "offsets": "character.detail1.safetensors", "error_mm": 0.04}
    ],
    "textures": {"base_color": "character.base_color.png", "normal": "character.normal.png", "eyes": "character.eyes.png", "subdiv": 1},
    "fit": {"placement": {"rotation": [[1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0], [0, 0, 1]], "translation": [0, 0, 0]}, "errors_mm": {}, "seconds": {}}
  }
}
```

Level 0 is anny's own mesh of 13,718 vertices. Levels 1 and 2 are one and two levels of Catmull-Clark subdivision of the quad mesh of the same vertices (`topology="anny-quads"`), with 54,848 and 219,368 vertices. The subdivision is `anny.utils.subdivision`, this repository's own implementation of Catmull-Clark as sparse linear operators (Apache-2.0); no outside library is involved. Because the subdivision is linear, the same operator carries every per-vertex quantity of level 0 to the finer levels:

- the rest mesh and the 52 ARKit morph targets, exactly;
- the skin weights, after which each vertex keeps its 12 strongest bones, renormalised (a file then keeps 4 or 8);
- the UVs, face-varying and bilinear inside each face of the level above, so that anny's atlas keeps its seams and its layout on every level.

Each offsets file (`opensculptboy/detail-offsets@1`) holds one float32 vector per vertex of its full level, in Anny's rest frame (metres, Z up), with the level and the topology in its metadata. The paths are relative to the card. `opensculptboy.detail.DetailModel` applies a level: it runs the Anny model, subdivides the rest mesh, adds the offsets after the blend shapes and skins the result with the subdivided weights. The offsets belong to the card's own parameter values; when a slider changes later, they stay where they were in the rest frame.

## Exports

`export_glb`, `export_vrm` and `opensculptboy export` take `detail_level` (`--detail`, default 0). Level 0 applies the card's level-0 offsets when it has them; a card without detail writes the same files as before, and `test/test_detail_layer.py` and the reference fingerprints of `test/test_gltf_export.py` guard that. Finer levels need the offsets of that level in the card.

| Mesh | Vertices | Triangles |
|---|---|---|
| level 0 (anny's mesh) | 13,718 | 27,420 |
| level 1 on the head and the neck, level 0 elsewhere | 27,644 | 55,272 |
| level 1 | 54,848 | 109,680 |
| level 2 on the head and the neck, level 1 elsewhere | 110,504 | 220,992 |
| level 2 | 219,368 | 438,720 |

A GLB file takes the full level. A VRM file keeps within the triangle budget of its version (`opensculptboy.export.budget`): when the full level is over it, the head and the neck alone take the level, and when that is still over it, the level steps down to one the card holds, with a `BudgetWarning`. So `--detail 1` gives VRM 1.0 files (70,000 triangles) level 1 on the head and the neck, and VRM 0.x files (32,000 triangles) level 0. `--budget off` keeps the full level. The scene extras record the level and the region in use. The fine meshes carry no `_ANNY_VERTEX` attribute, since their vertices are not Anny's. A card with an eye texture adds the eyeballs' primitive and material (see Textures), which raises the material count of a VRM file to 2 of its budget's 16 (1.0) or 8 (0.x).

## Textures

`--textures` (`opensculptboy.texture.bake.bake_fit`) bakes the target onto anny's UV atlas, on the mesh of the finest level, and `opensculptboy bake handoff/ --card character.json` (`bake_card`) bakes a fitted card again without fitting, from its own offsets and placement. For each texel, a ray along the fitted surface's normal (Embree, as in the viewer's bakes) finds the target's skin within 10 mm, on a triangle that faces the same way (on a thin part such as an ear, the nearest surface along the ray can be the other side). The texels of anny's eyeballs take the target's eyes in the same way. Texels that no ray reaches take the nearest reached value, which dilates every island to the edge of its texture, so that filtering and mipmaps do not pull in the background at the seams. The vertex colours of the target are never read: a source may keep other data in them, such as a game's masks or the albedo of hair cards.

| Map | Slot | Content |
|---|---|---|
| base colour | `base_color` | the target part's base colour texture at the hit (bilinear) |
| normal | `normal` | the target's shading normal in the tangent space of the fitted surface |
| roughness | `roughness` | the G channel of the target's metallic-roughness texture times its roughness factor (grey) |
| thickness | `thickness` | from the fitted mesh: the mean distance through it along the inward normal and six rays 30 degrees around it, over `thickness_mm` (30 mm), the eyeballs passed through (grey) |
| curvature | `curvature` | from the fitted mesh: the mean curvature (1 / radius, positive where convex) as 0.5 + 0.5 k / `curvature_per_m` (100 per metre), smoothed over 4 rings (grey) |

The shading normal is the target's own: its file normals (glTF `NORMAL`), which artists shape and smooth so that a surface reads differently from its bare geometry, with its normal map applied in its MikkTSpace frames, as Blender and glTF viewers apply it. The first real target showed why both matter: its file normals differ from its geometric normals by a median of 5.5 degrees and a 95th percentile of 22 degrees along the jaw and the lower face, and 10 to 20 degrees at the seam of the head and the body, so a bake of its geometry alone gave the jaw a bulge and the skin a plastic smoothness that the target does not show. The tangent space of the fitted surface is MikkTSpace too (`opensculptboy.texture.tangents`, an independent NumPy port of Mikkelsen's algorithm) on the mesh split at its UV seams with the normals of the welded mesh: the frames that the exporters write as `TANGENT` and that Blender computes, so that the map reads back as it was baked. On a 16,232-triangle head of a game character, the port agrees with Blender's tangents to 0.03 degrees (float32 rounding) and with its signs exactly, except next to the triangles of zero UV area, where Blender's own port is not consistent within a triangle; anny's meshes have none.

The maps are baked on the finest level (`detail.textures.subdiv` names it), and the coarser levels share the atlas, the head tile and the ear tile. Their surfaces turn differently from the finest level's, most of all at a pointed ear's tip, where level 1 folds by tens of degrees between triangles that level 2 rounds, so the finest level's map read in a coarser level's frames turns its normals by that difference and shows a crease along the coarse folds. The exporters therefore re-encode the card's normal maps for a coarser level or region (`opensculptboy.texture.levels.level_normals`): each texel that a triangle of the exported mesh covers is decoded in the baked level's frame at the same UV and encoded in the exported mesh's frame, so that both levels shade alike at rest. On the first real target, level 1 read the ear tile's map 9.4 degrees (mean) and 21.4 degrees (p95) off the bake's aim within 4 mm of the ear's tip, and 5.9 and 16.4 degrees over the rest of the ear; re-encoded, it reads 4.9 and 9.9 degrees off at the tip and 1.8 and 5.7 degrees over the rest (level 2: 1.1 and 2.8 degrees at the tip). What remains on level 1 is the difference of the points that one UV lands on in both levels. Textures passed to an exporter by hand are written as they are.

### Tiles

A 4096-texel atlas gives anny's head about 4.6 texels per millimetre of skin, as much as the target's 2048-texel head texture, so its detail would be resampled at about the rate it has. Three tiles therefore give parts of the body textures of their own, each drawn as a primitive of the body mesh with a material of its own (the same vertices, morph targets and skin, so that the bones, the look-at and the expressions are unchanged):

| Tile | Slots | Size | Covers | Layout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eyes (`opensculptboy.texture.eyes`) | `eyes` | 1024 by 512 | both eyeballs | the box of the atlas around them, through a texture transform |
| head (`opensculptboy.texture.tiles`) | `head_base_color`, `head_normal`, `head_roughness` | 2048 by 4096 | the head's UV island: the face, the scalp, the ears and the upper neck, 3,116 of anny's quads | the island's box of the atlas, through a texture transform |
| ears (`opensculptboy.texture.ears`) | `ears_base_color`, `ears_normal`, `ears_roughness`, `ears_thickness`, `ears_curvature` | 2048 by 1024 | the outer ears, on levels 1 and 2 | UVs of their own, unwrapped from the fitted ears |

The eye and head tiles keep anny's UVs and carry their box as a texture transform: `KHR_texture_transform` in glTF and VRM 1.0 (on every texture of the material), and the ST vector of `_MainTex` in VRM 0.x, which MToon 0.x applies to every texture and three-vrm and UniVRM read back as the same transform; a reader without `KHR_texture_transform` shows the whole tile squeezed onto its part. The head tile holds about 6.3 texels per millimetre, 1.35 times the target's head texture. The head's island ends at UV seams, so the border between the skin and the head materials follows a seam of the atlas; the triangles of the island are found by their UV centroids (`on_head_tile`), which works on every level and on the head region's meshes.

Anny's eyeballs cover about 52 by 52 texels each of a 2048-texel atlas, too few for an iris, so the bake also writes an eye texture (`character.eyes.png`, slot `eyes`, 1024 by 512 texels; `opensculptboy.texture.eyes`): the box of the atlas around both eyeballs, padded and widened to 2 by 1, baked from the target's eyes at about nine times the atlas's resolution along each side. The exports then draw the eyeballs as a second primitive of the body mesh (the same vertices, morph targets and skin, so that the eye bones, the look-at and the expressions are unchanged) with a material of their own, whose texture carries the box as a texture transform: `KHR_texture_transform` in glTF and VRM 1.0 (on the base, shade and normal textures of the MToon material), and the ST vector of `_MainTex` in VRM 0.x, which MToon 0.x applies to every texture and three-vrm and UniVRM read back as the same transform. The files keep one UV set, and a reader without `KHR_texture_transform` shows the whole eye texture squeezed onto the eyeballs. A card with the eyes of `opensculptboy.eyes` (`material.eyes`, `--eyes`) draws its eyes differently: smooth eyeballs with a cornea shell and a caruncle replace anny's eyeballs, and the baked eye texture is carried over to their own eye texture, its iris centred on the gaze and brought to the card's pupil and iris sizes and colour; [eyes.md](eyes.md) describes them.

Anny's atlas lays out its round ears, and a fit that reaches a pointed ear stretches the texels of the tip: on the first real target, the tip's texels on the head tile came to 1.5 per millimetre (5th percentile) against 3.5 at the median, and the bake showed streaks there. For levels 1 and 2, the ear tile unwraps each outer ear (the faces whose ear weight, `opensculptboy.fit.register.ear_weights`, is over 0.5: a disk of 6,557 vertices per ear on level 2) from the fitted rest surface of the card's finest level by a least-squares conformal map (Levy et al. 2002) refined by 20 as-rigid-as-possible iterations (Liu et al. 2008), with one scale for both ears, the left ear (+X) in the left half and each turned with its long side along v. Catmull-Clark numbers the vertex points of a level first, so vertex `i` of level 1 is vertex `i` of level 2, and one table of UVs per vertex of the finest level (`<card>.ear_uvs.safetensors`, `material.ears.uvs`) serves both levels; `with_ear_uvs` appends the ears' UVs after the atlas's and points the ear triangles at them, and the exporters draw them with the ear tile's material. Level 0 keeps anny's UVs. The tip of a pointed ear is a cone far from developable, where LSCM and ARAP turn triangles over: on level 2 of the real replica 179 of 25,968, and 20 of the 115 within 4 mm of the tip, whose texels overlapped others and whose tangent frames turned over, so that the bake showed crumpled patches of colour and shading at the tip. `untangle` therefore lays the flipped triangles and a few rings around them out again as a Tutte embedding (the whole disk with its border on a circle as the last resort), then takes ARAP steps that stop short of turning any triangle over or shrinking it below 5 % of its area on the surface; the map has no flipped triangle, and the normal map at the tip decodes to within 1.1 degrees of what the bake aimed for (mean; 8.0 before). On the first real target, the ear tile holds about 5.9 texels per millimetre at the median (6.7 before the untangling, which packs the tip's texels closer) and 4.5 at the 5th percentile (8.2 by area) before it, against 3.5 and 2.0 on the target's own ears. The ear islands are new UV seams; the normals stay welded across them.

The bake casts 2 by 2 rays per texel on the head tile and 3 by 3 on the ear tile, spread over the texel, and averages what they hit, so that the edges of colour, the rims of thin parts and the borders of the islands do not stair-step; the atlas takes one ray per texel. The bake of the first real target on level 2 with a 4096 atlas, the 2048 by 4096 head tile and the thickness and curvature maps took 136 s and 5.1 GB of memory with one ray per texel; the supersampled tiles add their rays to that.

## The skin material

A card may carry a skin material (`Character.material`, character schema 3; `opensculptboy.material.SkinMaterial`): the textures above as paths relative to the card, and the values that a renderer needs to rebuild the skin. `--textures` and `opensculptboy bake` write the textures' paths, and `--material skin.json` lays further values over them. Cards without the section keep their schema (1 or 2) and export exactly as before.

```json
"material": {
  "base_color": "character.base_color.png", "normal": "character.normal.png", "roughness": "character.roughness.png",
  "thickness": "character.thickness.png", "thickness_mm": 30.0, "curvature": "character.curvature.png", "curvature_per_m": 100.0,
  "roughness_factor": 1.0, "specular": 1.0, "specular_color": [1, 1, 1], "ior": 1.5, "normal_scale": 1.0,
  "subsurface": {"weight": 0.1, "radius_mm": [5.4, 2.1, 1.2], "ior": 1.4, "anisotropy": 0.0, "method": "random_walk_skin"},
  "head": {"base_color": "character.head_base_color.png", "normal": "character.head_normal.png", "roughness": "character.head_roughness.png"},
  "ears": {"uvs": "character.ear_uvs.safetensors", "base_color": "character.ears_base_color.png", "normal": "character.ears_normal.png", "...": "..."},
  "eyes": {"iris_color": [0.2, 0.1, 0.04], "pupil_mm": 3.5, "iris_tint": 0.0, "cornea": true, "caruncle": true}
}
```

The subsurface radius is the mean free path per RGB channel in millimetres (Blender's Subsurface Radius times its Subsurface Scale), and `method` names Blender's methods (`random_walk_skin`, `random_walk`, `burley`). The optional `eyes` object holds the eyes of [eyes.md](eyes.md): the iris colour and pattern, the pupil and iris sizes, the limbal ring, the sclera, the cornea and the caruncle; a section with nothing but `eyes` keeps the exporters' own skin.

**glTF.** The skin, head and ear materials are metallic-roughness materials (metallic 0, the roughness texture in `metallicRoughnessTexture`, read by its G channel) with `KHR_materials_specular` and `KHR_materials_ior`, and every primitive carries MikkTSpace `TANGENT`s when the file has a normal texture. glTF has no ratified subsurface extension: `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` is a release candidate for thin translucent surfaces such as leaves, `KHR_materials_volume` needs transmission, and the proposed `KHR_materials_scatter`, which would replace `KHR_materials_volume_scatter`, is still under review; an opaque skin under them would render as translucent in the viewers that support them. The export therefore keeps the subsurface settings in each skin material's extras, under `opensculptboy_skin` (format `opensculptboy/skin@1`): `subsurface` as in the card, and `thickness` and `curvature` as texture references (`index`, `texCoord`) with their scales (`max_mm`, `max_per_m`), for real-time engines that shade skin with a thickness map (translucency through the ears) and a pre-integrated lookup by curvature (Penner 2011). The head material reads the atlas's thickness and curvature maps, and the ear material its own. Blender's importer builds the Principled BSDF from the material and its extensions and keeps the extras as custom properties; `src/opensculptboy/export/blender_skin.py`, which needs nothing but Blender's own Python, then sets the subsurface:

```bash
blender --python src/opensculptboy/export/blender_skin.py -- character.glb   # import the file and set its skin
```

Inside Blender, `exec` the script and call `apply_skin_materials()` after an import (`apply_skin_materials("character.glb")` reads the settings from the file when the extras did not come along). It sets the subsurface method, Subsurface Weight, Subsurface Radius (normalised to its largest channel), Subsurface Scale (the largest channel in scene units), Subsurface IOR and Subsurface Anisotropy; Blender 4 and later take the subsurface colour from the base colour.

**VRM.** MToon has no subsurface scattering, so the VRM export bakes a skin ramp (`opensculptboy.export.skin_ramp`): for each channel, the diffuse falloff against N.L of a sphere of 20 mm radius (the scale of the nose, the lips and the ears) whose light spreads along the surface with the radial distribution of Burley's normalised diffusion profile at the channel's radius, mixed with a Lambert falloff by the subsurface weight; MToon's one ramp (`shadingShiftFactor`, `shadingToonyFactor`) and its shade colour per channel are fitted to the three falloffs by a grid search and least squares. The shade colour factor is the fitted ratio (times the base colour factor), over the base colour texture as the shade texture. It is an approximation: MToon has one ramp shape for all channels, no light through thin parts and no blur of the texture, and one curvature stands for the whole skin. Scattering of a few millimetres changes the falloff of a face little: the material above gets the shade ratio (0.011, 0.003, 0.001), shift -0.5 and toony 0.5, a Lambert ramp with a faint red terminator, and a subsurface weight of 1 gives (0.13, 0.02, 0.00). The ramp follows the material; it does not add the lighter, softer shade of a stylised toon skin, which the cards without a skin material keep. VRM files hold the base colour and the normal map of the skin, the head tile and the ear tile, and no roughness, thickness or curvature maps.

## Accuracy

`test/test_reference_fit.py` fits a synthetic target: anny with random phenotypes, 12 random local changes, 10 random face shapes and a small pose, plus smooth random offsets of 3 mm RMS on level 0 and 0.8 mm RMS on level 1, evaluated on level 2, moved by a random rigid placement and remeshed (the vertices slide within the surface, and vertex clustering on a 5 mm grid merges them into 53,192 vertices and 107,818 triangles, without the eyeballs, the tongue and the insides of the mouth and the eye sockets). The landmarks carry 1 mm of noise. The errors, in millimetres, are the distances from the fitted vertices to the target surface (forward) and from 20,000 target vertices to the fitted surface (reverse):

| Stage | Forward mean | Forward p95 | Reverse mean | Reverse p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parameters alone | 0.989 | 2.535 | 1.053 | 2.715 |
| level 0 | 0.019 | 0.000 | 0.397 | 1.019 |
| level 1 | 0.005 | 0.000 | 0.129 | 0.327 |
| level 2 | 0.003 | 0.000 | 0.028 | 0.088 |

The refinement ends by moving the vertices onto the target (see the pipeline), so the forward errors are close to 0 by construction and the reverse errors carry the measure; before that step, the reverse means were 0.488 mm on level 0, 0.133 mm on level 1 and 0.072 mm on level 2. Some of the random local changes act on targets that the face shapes share, which the fit leaves to the face shapes, so the parameters alone fit no better than before.

The test holds level 1 under 0.5 mm in both directions. Without landmarks, the same target fits to 0.33 mm (forward) and 0.46 mm (reverse) on level 0. The fit to level 1 takes about 75 s (parameters 38 s, level 0 8 s, level 1 30 s), and level 2 adds about 180 s and needs about 4 GB of memory.

A synthetic target is the best case: anny's parameter space holds its shape, and the residuals are smooth. A real character leaves larger residuals in the parameters and leans more on the detail layer.

### Measuring a fit

The errors above are optimistic: the projection of the last stage moves the vertices onto the target, so the forward errors are close to 0 by construction, and the reverse errors left out the reference vertices that do not see out of the reference. `opensculptboy.fit.metrics.measure` measures a fitted mesh as it is, with no mask, no visibility test and no distance cut-off: the signed distance of every skin vertex of the fit to the reference (positive: the fit lies outside), of every reference vertex to the fitted surface, and the angle between the fitted vertex normals and the reference's normals at the closest point, both its geometric normals and the normals its file shades with (a GLB file's `NORMAL` attribute, which `load_handoff` now reads as `HandoffPart.normals` and `Reference.file_normals`). It reports mean, signed mean, p95 and maximum per region (face, jaw with the skin under the chin, ears, scalp, neck, the seam of the head and body parts, body, hands, and anny's mouth and eye cavities, which a closed skin has no counterpart for). The ears come from the handoff's `masks.ear` when it has one (`Handoff.mask`, `Reference.regions["ear"]`). It also reports, per region, the distance of each fitted triangle's centroid to the reference and the share over 1 mm (`face_mm`, `faces_over_1mm`: a triangle that bridges a gap, such as skin stretched from the back of an ear to the head, has its corners on the reference and its middle in the air), the share of fitted vertices at an edge folded by more than 60 degrees (`folded_over_60deg`, which the rim of a thin ear also counts) and of those folded by 30 degrees more than the reference within 3 mm of their closest point (`folds_not_in_reference`, `metrics.fold_near`: the crumples and spikes of the fit alone), the share of fitted vertices whose normal turns from the reference's by more than 90 degrees (`facing_away`), and, with an ear mask, the reverse measure of the reference vertices within 15 mm of the mask and outside it (`ears_surround`), and, with an attachment line, the fitted vertices whose closest point lies within 3 mm of it (`ears_root`: their count, the two shares above among them, and `defects`, the share with either; a crack along the root of the ear shows there as folded and flipped slivers). The reference's own folds are those of its welded surface (`Reference.folds`), since a source splits its vertices at its UV seams and where a piece of its mesh is stitched to another. `fit_levels` records these measures before and after the projection in `FitResult.measures` (on the synthetic target, level 1 measures 0.21 mm forward and 0.13 mm reverse before the projection and 0.19 and 0.13 mm after it, against 0.005 and 0.129 mm in the table above; the test holds both under 0.5 mm), and `opensculptboy.fit.views` renders grey-shaded pictures and heat maps of them (Embree ray casting).

Measured this way, the level-2 fit of the real target lies within 0.025 mm (mean) and 0.09 mm (p95) of the replica on the jaw, reverse; the heavier, squarer jaw seen in the textured comparison is not a shape error. The replica's file normals differ from its geometric normals by 2 degrees (median) and 17 degrees (p95) on the head, most along the jaw line and the throat: shaded with its geometric normals the replica itself shows the hard jaw. Its look therefore needs the file normals in the normal-map bake. Two geometry issues are real: the ears (reverse mean 0.14 mm, p95 0.47 mm on level 2, with crumples where the back of the pointed tip meets the head, at the front of the helix root and at the notch below the concha), and the facets of the replica's coarse triangles, which level 2 copies under the chin and along the jaw. `FitOptions.target_smooth` and `target_shading` (fit the Phong tessellation of the reference, curved by its file normals) and `FitOptions.reverse="surface"` (the reverse term on the closest points of the fitted triangles) are experimental and off by default: on the real target they rounded the facets but left the jaw's shading to the normals and made the ears worse.

## Limits

- Hair, eyelashes, brows, teeth, the tongue and the eyeballs are left out of the fit; they attach to the fitted body in a later step. Clothing is left out too.
- The inside of the mouth and the eye sockets follow the skin around them and do not fit the target's.
- An ear far from anny's shape (a pointed ear with deep folds) needs the ear outline landmarks; with them it reaches its outline and its main folds on the detail levels, without them it reaches the outline alone. An ear that stands off from the head needs `masks.ear` as well, without which the skin of the head stretches across the gap behind it. The split trusts the mask and anny's own outer ear to part along the attachment line: a mask that holds much of the head's skin around the root, or leaves out part of the ear, moves the line, and `FitOptions.ear_attachment_reach` (off by default; on the synthetic target 4 mm covered the head around the ears better and folded the root more) lets both parts share a strip along it.
- The body landmarks are joint centres of another rig, so they guide the pose and the proportions loosely. The face landmarks carry most of the placement.
- The fit assumes the contract's axes and an upright figure. A target in a very different pose from anny's (arms raised high, crouching) needs landmarks on the elbows, the wrists and the knees.
- The offsets are tied to the card's values: changing a slider afterwards moves the parametric body under fixed offsets.
- The bake carries the target's base colour, roughness and shading normals, not its other maps (specular, cavity or subsurface masks); the subsurface values come from the card. The tiles' resolution is fixed per tile (the eyes 1024 by 512, the head 2048 by 4096 at `--head-size 4096`, the ears 2048 by 1024).
- The ear tile's unwrap of a pointed ear packs the tip's texels closer (on the real replica the tip within 4 mm holds about 3.4 texels per millimetre at the median against 5.9 over the ear), since the tip is a cone that no flat map spreads evenly without folding.
- The fit needs SciPy (the `viewer` or `examples` extra), and the texture bake needs `embreex` (the `viewer` extra).
